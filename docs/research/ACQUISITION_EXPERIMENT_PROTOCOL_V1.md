# SimpleWay One — Acquisition Experiment Protocol V1

Status: LAB / METHOD RESEARCH — NOT CORE LAW
Date: 2026-09-22

## Purpose
Define a versioned protocol for testing acquisition hypotheses before promoting them into SimpleWay Method or executable CORE contracts.

## Epistemic rule
Observed performance is evidence; it is not automatically learning, retention, transfer, generalization, autonomy, proficiency, or mastery.

The CEFR is used as an external framework for communicative capabilities and activities, not as a cognitive acquisition theory. Its action-oriented model distinguishes reception, production, interaction and mediation and uses can-do descriptors.

Learning-science findings such as retrieval-practice effects are treated as evidence informing hypotheses, not as immutable product rules.

## Candidate acquisition dimensions
1. Exposure
2. Comprehension
3. Retrieval
4. Production
5. Interaction
6. Mediation
7. Transfer
8. Retention
9. Generalization
10. Autonomy

These dimensions may overlap and are not asserted to form a universal linear sequence.
## Experimental unit
Every experiment must specify:
- experiment ID and version;
- hypothesis and competing/null hypothesis;
- target capability;
- Language Pack(s) and exact versions;
- learner population and relevant prior knowledge;
- intervention/activity;
- comparison/control condition when appropriate;
- representation(s) and support available;
- evaluation layers;
- evidence collected;
- immediate observation point;
- delayed observation point(s) when retention is claimed;
- transfer/generalization context when those are claimed;
- stopping/exclusion rules defined before analysis;
- result and uncertainty;
- promotion decision: KEEP / ADAPT / TEST AGAIN / REJECT.

## Evidence chain
`CAPABILITY → REALIZATION → ACTIVITY → EVALUATION → EVIDENCE → PROGRESS EVENT → ACQUISITION STATE`

An experiment may inspect any link, but must not silently skip provenance.

## Claim boundaries
- Retrieval claim requires an attempt to retrieve, not mere re-exposure.
- Retention claim requires a delayed observation; the delay is recorded, not globally fixed by CORE.
- Transfer claim requires a meaningfully changed context, cue, representation or task.
- Generalization requires evidence across more than one novel case/context.
- Autonomy requires reduced/absent scaffolding relative to the supported condition.
- No single event licenses a universal `mastered = true` claim.
## Initial experiment registry
EXP-21 Delayed Retention — compare immediate success with delayed retrieval.
EXP-22 Support Withdrawal — measure performance after scaffold reduction/removal.
EXP-23 Context Transfer — move the same capability into a novel communicative context.
EXP-24 Representation Transfer — test movement between script/audio/visual or other representations where language-appropriate.
EXP-25 Retrieval vs Restudy — compare active retrieval with additional exposure under controlled conditions.
EXP-26 Interaction — test capability during reciprocal meaning negotiation rather than isolated response.
EXP-27 Mediation — test making meaning accessible to another person or across a barrier where appropriate.
EXP-28 Generalization Boundary — probe how far observed learning extends to novel cases before failure.

## Cross-language requirement
No pedagogical mechanism becomes universal merely because it succeeds in English or HNK. Promotion toward SimpleWay Method should seek replication across meaningfully different Language Packs when the mechanism claims language-independence.

## Promotion ladder
`HYPOTHESIS → PROTOCOL → PILOT → RESULT → REPLICATION → METHOD CANDIDATE → CROSS-LANGUAGE VALIDATION → PROMOTION REVIEW`

CORE promotion requires a separate architecture review. Method evidence alone does not justify hard-coding a pedagogical theory into CORE.

## External evidence anchors
- Council of Europe CEFR: action-oriented language use, can-do descriptors, reception, production, interaction and mediation.
- Roediger & Karpicke / retrieval-practice literature: retrieval can improve later retention relative to additional study under studied conditions; feedback and design conditions matter.

External findings constrain and inform experiments but do not replace direct validation of SimpleWay implementations.
